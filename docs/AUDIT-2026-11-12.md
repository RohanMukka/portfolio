# Audit #5: measured, and compared with today's best

Branch `rohan/hero-redesign-wip` at `614916c` (same as origin), audited
2026-11-12 on a production build. The owner's brief: top in the world, nothing
protected, measure everything, compare with current award-level sites, rank
the gaps, propose one next step.

## How it was measured

| What | How |
|:-|:-|
| Build, budgets | `vite build`, `npm run perf` (all 14 pass) |
| Lighthouse 12 | Median of 3 runs, phone and desktop presets, build served with gzip |
| Frames | puppeteer + Chrome on this machine's GPU (Intel Arc 140T), one continuous scroll of the whole page, every frame's length recorded and grouped by section; repeated with the CPU slowed 4× |
| Phone | 390×844 emulation, DPR 3, real touch events, CPU at 1× and 4×. Not a real phone |
| Slow network | Phone profile, 4× CPU, slow 4G (1.6 Mbps, 150 ms) and slow 3G (400 kbps, 400 ms) |
| Walk | 28 desktop and 25 phone screenshots, every section; a case study on both; light theme |
| Links | Every URL in `src/` and `index.html` fetched |
| Claims | All 19 READMEs downloaded and each number and noun on the site searched for in them |
| References | The same Lighthouse run and a first-screen probe on nine reference sites |

Not measured: real phones, Safari, Firefox, screen readers.

**Layout shift:** phone 0. Desktop **0.15 at 1440×900 and 0.12 at 1920×1080**
(over the 0.1 "good" line): at about 0.6 s the fonts arrive and the hero's
copy block moves 39 px while the intro still covers it. Nobody sees it, but
Chrome's field data and DevTools count it. Lighthouse misses it because at its
1350×940 window the text happens not to rewrap.

**Accessibility:** axe finds nothing; headings and landmarks are right; focus
is visible on the first 16 Tab stops. No skip link.

**Links:** all answer. The EduLens demo timed out at 40 s on the first request
and answered in 0.3 s on the second (Render's cold start).

**Claims:** every number in the eight case studies and the project list is in

- IPMS's "I built the request, tracking and evaluation modules" comes from the

**The résumé PDF** (unchanged since September) says "Software Engineer…
blockchain-based systems… currently pursuing a Master's", lists BEneFIT, IPMS
and the Diagnostic System, none of the eight featured projects, and uses the
OU email. It is the hero's first link.

**Source:** `tsc` passes, but `strict` is off (986 errors with it on, 178 in

**Live site:** `main` is 44 commits behind. Lighthouse phone 68 / 90 / 75 /

## Walk

- **Hero:** role, claim and four links are on the first screen on both widths.
  On a phone the particle name is the softest thing on the screen.
- **About:** 5,000 px of desktop scroll (5.5 screens) for about 110 words.
  The first project is 5,900 px below the top.
- **Work tour:** captions are clean. Between stops there are full screens
  with no text at all. NexusRAG's caption says the same thing twice (the fact
  line, then the description).
- **Case study:** 242 words, a box diagram, three numbers. No results table,
  no chart, nothing from the project's real output except EduLens's clip.
- **Skills:** the sentence is the résumé's full-stack list (Java, C++,
  Angular, Firebase, Jenkins, Kubernetes, AWS) and names none of PyTorch,
  FastAPI, Kafka, Redis, Airflow, MLflow or ChromaDB, which the featured
  projects are built on. A chip grid under it repeats the same tools
  (with MATLAB, PyCharm, Windows). 3,100 px of scroll.
- **Credentials:** clear; six certificates at equal weight.
- **Contact:** the ring is the best moment on the page. "my inbox is always
  open" is filler.
- **Footer:** a newsletter that doesn't exist, five icon-only links, three
  third-party cards (one down), and the name a fourth time (hero, navbar,
  footer brand, footer wordmark) with the floating buttons over its last
  letter.

What this says:

- **Performance and accessibility engineering: already ahead of every award
  3D site measured**, by a wide margin, at a tenth of the weight. A render
  loop that sleeps, a self-measuring page and zero axe findings are things
  none of them have. Awwwards' developer jury scored the 2026 winner 7.0 on
  accessibility and 7.2 on performance.
- **Art direction: behind them.** Their 3D is a modelled, authored world
  (Parpeix's sculpted room, Simon's Blender island with 78,400 grass blades,
  compressed textures, a phone quality preset, MIT source and video devlogs).
  Ours is particles with bloom: well executed, widely seen, and it says "I
  can do WebGL", which is not the role. Awwwards weights design 40% and
  creativity 20%.
- **Substance: behind the engineers.** Eugene Yan's home page counts 212
  posts, 31 talks and 19 prototypes with demos. Josh Comeau's pages teach
  with interactive widgets. Karpathy's is a timeline of things shipped. Our
  deepest project page is 242 words. A senior engineer judges that, not the
  particles.
- **Storytelling:** the award sites make the work the experience. Here the
  3D is a backdrop to the work, and the two longest scroll stretches (About,
  Skills) carry the least information.
