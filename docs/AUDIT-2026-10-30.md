#### 01 · ReviewGuard

**Hook.** A fake-review detector for YelpCHI that first proved the benchmark
was leaking, then reported what was left.

**Architecture and data flow.** Two branches. Text: TF-IDF reduced with
truncated SVD. Behaviour: reviewer and business features. Branches fuse into
a classifier trained with focal loss for the class imbalance; a GNN variant
adds the reviewer to business graph. Every model is scored on a 2D grid: split
(business-disjoint or reviewer-disjoint) × feature regime (text, behaviour,
graph, all), so each number says what it could and couldn't see.

**Hardest problem.** The numbers looked too good. A single feature, the raw
timestamp, scored 0.965 AUC; a business's fake rate alone scored 0.951. Both
are label leakage, not detection. The date column was dropped, the shipped
behavioural features were shown to be noise (≤ 0.005 correlation) and
excluded, and every model was re-run on splits that keep businesses unseen.
Then the GNN's gain (0.816 vs 0.706 without a graph) was traced to degree
counts: two counts alone recover 0.788.

**Bullets.**
- Exposed two leakage paths in a standard benchmark: 0.965 AUC from the
  timestamp alone, 0.951 from per-business fake rate.
- Reported 0.805 business-level AUC on businesses never seen in training, under splits built
  so no model can memorise a business.
- Showed about three quarters of a GNN's apparent gain over a graph-free
  model comes from two node degree counts (0.788 of 0.816, against 0.706).

#### 02 · NexusRAG

**Hook.** A RAG system that retrieves with both meaning and keywords, streams
grounded answers, and grades every answer for faithfulness.

**Architecture and data flow.** Ingest: PDFs, CSVs and web pages, chunked and
embedded (OpenAI embeddings) into ChromaDB, with a parallel BM25 index over
the same chunks. Query: a rule-based router classifies the query (semantic,
structured, conversational, follow-up) without an LLM call; retrieval runs
dense and BM25 in parallel, merges with Reciprocal Rank Fusion and
passes the top chunks to GPT-4o-mini; tokens stream to a Next.js 14 client.
Post-answer: RAGAS metrics and a hallucination check score the answer against
its sources, and the scores land on an evaluation dashboard. Five turns of
memory carry follow-ups.

**Hardest problem.** Dense retrieval missed exact identifiers (codes, names)
and BM25 missed paraphrase. Weighted score blending was brittle because the
two scores aren't on one scale. RRF fuses *ranks*, not scores, so neither
retriever needs calibrating; the cost is a second index, held under 50 ms.

**Bullets.**
- About 15 to 20% better recall on keyword-heavy queries from RRF (the README's estimate; `[measure: recall@5 on the eval set]`).
- Under 50 ms added by the parallel BM25 index.
- Every answer scored for faithfulness before a user can trust it; `[measure:
  share of answers flagged by the hallucination check on the sample corpus]`.

#### 03 · MLFlowForge

**Hook.** A fraud-detection MLOps pipeline that won't promote a model below
0.70 average precision and retrains itself when the data drifts.

**Architecture and data flow.** Airflow orchestrates: validated ingest of
284k card transactions → feature engineering → XGBoost and PyTorch training
(SMOTE on the training split only) → MLflow tracking and registry →
promotion gate (AP ≥ 0.70 on held-out data; previous production version
archived) → FastAPI serving with SHAP explanations. Evidently, Wasserstein
distance and PSI watch live inputs; crossing a threshold triggers the
retraining DAG. GitHub Actions runs the tests.

**Hardest problem.** Fraud is a fraction of a percent of rows, so accuracy is
meaningless and naïve oversampling leaks. SMOTE applied before the split puts
synthetic neighbours of test rows into training and inflates every metric.
Resampling only inside the training split, and gating on average precision
(which ignores the easy negatives), makes the promoted number honest.

**Bullets.**
- Quality gate: no model reaches production under AP 0.70; promotion and
  archival are automatic.
- Three independent drift signals (Wasserstein, PSI, share of drifted
  columns) trigger retraining without a human.
- 284k transactions, end to end from raw CSV to an explained prediction over
  HTTP; `[measure: p95 /predict latency]`.

#### 04 · SafeFlow

**Hook.** A five-agent crowd-safety command center that reroutes stadium
crowds, dispatches stewards and checks its own past decisions before acting.

**Architecture and data flow.** A FastAPI gateway in front of a Google ADK
hierarchy on `gemini-2.5-flash`: a Supervisor `SequentialAgent` runs a
five-stage playbook (analyze, strategize, reroute, dispatch, broadcast)
through Analysis, Mitigation, Communications and Post-Mortem `LlmAgent`s.
Agents act only through tools that call REST endpoints; the gateway owns
every MongoDB Atlas write. Reasoning streams to a React command center over
SSE; stadium state is pushed over a WebSocket every 0.5 s and drawn on a
React Three Fiber stadium. Every request and tool call is traced with
OpenTelemetry into Arize Phoenix. Operational actions wait on a
human-in-the-loop approval endpoint.

**Hardest problem.** Letting an LLM change the world safely. Three decisions:
agents never touch the database directly (the gateway validates every
write); actions pause at an approval gate; and the Analysis Agent queries
Phoenix's `/v1/spans` for how past dispatches performed before choosing a
new one, so it learns from its own traces instead of a prompt.

**Bullets.**
- Five agents with single responsibilities, sequenced by a supervisor; every
  tool call traced end to end.
- Self-introspection: the agent reads its own OpenTelemetry history before
  acting.
- Two live channels: SSE for reasoning, WebSocket at 2 Hz for state; human
  approval required before any operational write.

#### 05 · EduLens AI (live demo)

**Hook.** Students explain a concept in their own words; an agent pipeline
diagnoses the misconception and adapts the next question.

**Architecture and data flow.** React 18 client → gatekeeper agent (rejects
plagiarised or off-topic answers) → diagnostic agent (six misconception
types) → Bloom's taxonomy classifier (four levels) → remediation engine →
adaptive next question; SM-2 schedules review. Llama 3.1 8B on Groq;
PostgreSQL via Drizzle; teachers watch a live classroom over WebSockets.

**Hardest problem.** Grading free text consistently. Multiple choice is easy
to grade and tests recognition; explanations test understanding but need a
model you can check. The diagnosis was validated against human grading
before it drove anything adaptive.

**Bullets.**
- 83.3% misconception diagnosis accuracy against human grading.
- 3.2 s average feedback latency on Llama 3.1 8B (the README also says "under 2.0 s"; fix the README first).
- 6 misconception types × 4 Bloom's levels scored per answer; live for
  teachers over WebSockets.

#### 06 · StreamSense

**Hook.** A clickstream pipeline that ingests 5,000+ events a second through
Kafka and survives consumer failure without losing a count.

**Architecture and data flow.** Producers → Kafka (6 partitions, LZ4) →
consumer group with rolling-window aggregation → hot path in Redis 7 (keys
expire at twice the window; sub-millisecond reads for the dashboard's 2 s
poll) and cold path in PostgreSQL 16 → FastAPI → Next.js 14 + Recharts.
Malformed events go to a dead-letter topic.

**Hardest problem.** Exactly-once is expensive and this pipeline doesn't need
it: the aggregates are counters where a rare duplicate is noise.
At-least-once with idempotent-enough aggregation kept the design simple, and
a second consumer takes over a dead one's partitions.

**Bullets.**
- 5,000+ events/s ingested on 6 partitions.
- ~40% smaller messages with LZ4.
- Dashboard reads under 1 ms from Redis; failures isolated to a dead-letter
  queue instead of crashing consumers.

#### 07 · JobForge

**Hook.** A distributed task queue built from scratch on Redis Streams, with
priorities, retries, dependencies and a chaos mode.

**Architecture and data flow.** Producers write to one stream per priority →
async workers (3 × 4 in flight) claim with `XREADGROUP`; dead workers' jobs
are reclaimed with `XAUTOCLAIM` → failures go to a sorted set keyed by
next-retry time (exponential backoff), drained by a scheduler with
`ZRANGEBYSCORE` → after the retry limit, a dead-letter queue. Per-type rate
limits and job dependencies; FastAPI + WebSocket dashboard; chaos mode kills
workers to prove recovery.

**Hardest problem.** No double processing without a distributed lock.
Consumer groups give each message to exactly one consumer atomically;
pending-entry reclaim covers crashes. No lock service, no lease tuning.

**Bullets.**
- 0 distributed locks; atomic claims via consumer groups.
- O(log N) to find due retries regardless of queue size.
- High priority never waits behind a low-priority backlog (read order, not
  weights); `[measure: jobs/s at 12 in flight]`.

#### 08 · Multi-Agent Course Builder

**Hook.** Four AI agents, each its own Cloud Run service, research a topic,
judge the research and write a course over Google's A2A protocol.

**Architecture and data flow.** An orchestrator finds researcher, judge and
builder through A2A agent cards; research and judging loop in ADK until the
judge passes, then the builder runs. Service-to-service calls are signed by
a shared httpx client; card URLs are rewritten for deployed hosts.

**Hardest problem.** Agents as microservices means auth, discovery and
deployment per agent. Signed calls and card rewriting made remote agents
behave like local ones.

**Bullets.**
- 4 agents, 4 independently deployed services.
- A judge gates the builder: nothing is written from unvetted research.
- A2A discovery through agent cards, no hard-coded endpoints.
